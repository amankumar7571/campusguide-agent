# Autonomous Ordinance 2024-25 — Rules and Regulations (Ver. 1.2)

**Source:** https://www.old.kiet.edu/uploads/deanA/682580.pdf — "Rules & Regulations, Session 2024-25, Ver. 1.2", KIET Group of Institutions (An Autonomous Institute Affiliated to AKTU, Lucknow). Full PDF text fetched 11 November 2026.
**Regime:** Autonomous Ordinance 2024-25 — governs AUTONOMOUS batches (students admitted from session 2024-25 under autonomy). It does NOT govern the 2023–27 final-year batch (the "Affiliated Batch"), which remains governed by AKTU rules. Do NOT apply these rules to a final-year (2023–27) student's question as if they were that student's rules.
**Fetched:** 11 November 2026

## 1. Application and scope

Regime note: Autonomous Ordinance 2024-25 — autonomous batches only; not the 2023–27 Affiliated Batch (AKTU rules).

All academic programs of the institute are governed by the rules and regulations approved by the Academic Council of KIET Group of Institutions, Ghaziabad. These rules and regulations are applicable to all students of this institute from academic session 2024-25 onwards, as per the decision in the Academic Council meeting. The institution is approved by AICTE and affiliated to Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam Technical University (AKTU), Lucknow. KIET became an Autonomous Institution from session 2024-25. The institute has incorporated the key recommendations of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 into its curriculum design.

Key definitions: "Detained" means a student enrols in a course for a semester but, due to poor attendance, is not permitted to appear in the exam. A "Dropped" course is one a student drops from a specific semester within 2 weeks of the start of the semester. "Institute" means KIET Group of Institutions, Ghaziabad, Delhi-NCR. One credit is equivalent to one hour of teaching (lecture or tutorial) or two hours of practical work/field work per week per semester.

## 2. Programs offered

Undergraduate degree programs: Computer Science and Engineering (CSE), Computer Science (CS), Information Technology (IT), Computer Science and Information Technology (CSIT), Computer Science and Engineering (Artificial Intelligence) — CSE(AI), Computer Science and Engineering (Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning) — CSE(AIML), Mechanical Engineering (ME), Electrical and Electronics Engineering (EN), Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE), Electrical and Computer Engineering (ELCE), Computer Science and Engineering (Cyber Security) — CSE(CS), Computer Science and Engineering (Data Science) — CSE(DS), Electronics and Communication Engineering (VLSI Design and Technology) — ECE(VLSI), Advanced Mechatronics and Industrial Automation (AM&IA).

Postgraduate degree programs: Master of Technology in CSE — M.Tech (CSE), Master of Computer Applications (MCA), Master of Business Administration (MBA).

## 3. Rules of admission

The Institute adopts procedures to admit students to the different Undergraduate/Postgraduate Programs as prescribed by AKTU, Lucknow, and follows norms for eligibility as per the affiliating university / state government norms.

### Change of branch (B.Tech only)

Change of branch may be allowed against vacant seats in two stages: in the first year, after the last date of admission to the B.Tech first semester, based on merit of the entrance examination on a vacant seat; in the second year, on the basis of merit at the B.Tech first year examination for those who pass without any carry over paper. After the change, the number of students in a branch shall neither increase over the intake approved by AICTE nor decrease below 75% of the intake approved by AICTE. Change of branch is not applicable to candidates admitted into the second year of B.Tech through the lateral entry scheme. If permitted, a branch change is effective from the third semester of the B.Tech program.

### Fees

Tuition fees and other fees for UG and PG programs are as per the approval of the Fees Regulatory Body from time to time, with payment dates declared in advance. Examination fees are separate, semester-wise. Summer semester fees are additional, calculated on the credits opted. Minor degree fees are separate for course registration and examination. Caution money deposits are refunded after successful completion of the course or after cancelling admission; unless there is any recovery, no deduction is made. If a candidate does not apply for a refund within 3 complete financial years after actually leaving the institution, or within 3 complete financial years after successful completion of the course, whichever is earlier, the caution money is transferred to the students aid fund.

## 4. Duration and credits

There are two semesters in each academic year: Odd Semester and Even Semester. B.Tech: minimum 8 semesters, maximum 14 semesters. B.Tech (admitted directly to second year / lateral entry): minimum 6 semesters, maximum 12 semesters. M.Tech, MBA and MCA: minimum 4 semesters, maximum 8 semesters.

Total credits to be earned for the award of the degree: B.Tech 160 credits; B.Tech (lateral entry) 115 credits; B.Tech with Honors 160 + 20 = 180 credits; B.Tech with a Minor 160 + 16 = 176 credits; M.Tech 80 credits; MBA 80 credits; MCA 80 credits.

A candidate who has failed twice in the first year due to any reason (either non-appearance or not being permitted to appear in semester examinations) shall not be allowed to continue studies further. If a student wishes to continue a third time in the first year, they may be allowed on the terms and conditions laid down by the University, but the maximum time allowed for completing the course remains the same.

## 9. Industrial training / internship

It is mandatory for each student (UG/PG) to undergo industrial internship.

- B.Tech: students before entering the 3rd semester are encouraged to work under Human Values / Unnat Bharat Abhiyan (UBA) / Indian Knowledge System (IKS) / NGO / rural organisation / government organisation working for the welfare of society as a Social Internship. Six weeks industrial internship must be completed before entering the 7th semester. This may include 2 weeks of social internship.
- MBA: six weeks summer internship program before entering the 3rd semester.
- MCA: six weeks summer internship program before entering the 3rd semester; after completion of training, the student submits a report to the Institute which forms part of the third semester examination.

## 10. Feedback by students

Student feedback is conducted by the Director Academics office. Faculty feedback by students (odd and even semester) is conducted on ERP two times; students having more than 60% attendance are allowed to fill the feedback forms, and it is mandatory for students to fill the feedback form. Curriculum feedback survey and course exit survey are also conducted on ERP before the end of the semester.

## 11. Attendance criteria

Regime note: Autonomous Ordinance 2024-25 — autonomous batches only; not the 2023–27 Affiliated Batch (AKTU rules).

Students are encouraged to maintain 100% attendance. Every student is required to attend all lectures, tutorials, practicals and other prescribed curricular and co-curricular activities.

- The attendance criteria for appearing in MSE1 is 70%, and for MSE2 and ESE it is 75%, course-wise. However, if there is only one MSE, the attendance criteria is 75%.
- Students not allowed to appear in ESE due to low attendance have to register for the courses in the summer semester.
- If a student wants to participate in any co-curricular / extracurricular activities in and outside the campus, they must get the leave approved in advance.
- Severe medical cases will be submitted to the Head of the Institute by individual departments for approval of up to 15% attendance relaxation. However, it is mandatory to complete 60% attendance.

## 12. Examination

A student must necessarily register separately for the End Semester Examinations (ESE) in all backlog courses and the new registered courses, as per the dates mentioned in the calendar. If a student gets detained in a course after exam registration, their exam registration for that course is treated as cancelled and they will have to register for the summer semester. Students are permitted to appear in a backlog exam of a course within the Vacation Exam and during the respective end semester examinations of subsequent academic sessions in which they got a backlog. Every student must fill in the online examination form as per the dates given in the academic calendar through the portal, and as per the notification given by the Registrar Office through the available portal.

All sets of question papers of each course are moderated by a duly constituted Moderation Committee, coordinated by the Chairman of the committee or their nominee.

## 13. Evaluation

Regime note: Autonomous Ordinance 2024-25 — autonomous batches only; not the 2023–27 Affiliated Batch (AKTU rules).

A student's performance in a semester is evaluated in two parts: (a) Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) and (b) End Semester Examination (ESE). CIE includes Continuous Assessments (CA), Mid Semester Examinations (MSE) and Attendance (ATT) in classes.

### Assessment pattern for B.Tech courses

- Theory / blended courses (lecture/tutorials) with ESE: Mid Semester Examination (MSE) 40%, Continuous Assessment (CA) 10%, End Semester Examination (ESE) 50%.
- Theory / blended courses without ESE: MSE 80%, CA 20%.
- Laboratory practical work / project with ESE: laboratory practical work (CA) 50%, ESE 50%.
- Laboratory practical work without ESE: laboratory practical work (CA) 100%.

Continuous Assessments are based on assignments/tutorials, quizzes/viva-voce, activity-based learning, collaborative learning, practical-based learning, problem-solving, group discussions and seminars, depending on the course. There are one or two Mid Semester Examinations (MSE1 and MSE2) as per the scheme for theory and blended courses; the MSE is pen-paper based or activity based.

### Mid-semester examination process

There are one or two MSEs in a semester on the dates given in the academic calendar. Pen-and-paper exams are conducted centrally by the Controller of Examinations, and activity-based exams by the respective Board of Studies. The examination schedule, seating plan, invigilation duty, hall tickets and list of students not permitted to appear in the MSE are published centrally by the Controller of Examinations. A centralised evaluation system is adopted; evaluated answer sheets are shown to students and then marks are uploaded on ERP within the deadline in the academic calendar.

### Make-up examination

If a student is absent in any one or both MSEs, they are permitted a make-up examination, provided they give prior information to the respective Dean of the department mentioning the reason for absence. Only genuine cases with prior permission are allowed. Only one make-up examination is conducted at the end of the semester covering the whole syllabus, and it is considered for the MSE in which the student was absent. If the student is absent in both MSE1 and MSE2, the make-up examination replaces only one of the MSEs.

### End semester examination process

The ESE is conducted by the Controller of Examinations at the end of each semester on the dates given in the academic calendar. The examination schedule is prepared 15 days before the ESE. Duration of exams is as per the teaching scheme. The examination schedule, seating plan, invigilation duty, hall tickets and list of detained students are published centrally by the Controller of Examinations. To ensure security inside the campus during ESE, the local police station is intimated.

### Evaluation of ESE

For theory courses, a centralised evaluation system is adopted, headed by the Professor In-charge of Digital Evaluation; for each evaluated bundle, sample copies are picked up for moderation. Practicals, viva-voce and projects are examined/evaluated through internal and external examiners as per the scheme.

## 14. Paper showing and grievance handling

The schedule for paper showing is published by the Controller of Examinations (CoE). According to the schedule, students should see the evaluated answer booklet of MSE/ESE. It is the responsibility of students to see the evaluated answer sheet and marks obtained after each MSE and ESE within the specified time. If a student fails to see it on the said date, they are not entitled to file a grievance after the prescribed date. After seeing the answer booklet, they can apply to the CoE in the grievance format; the CoE appoints a senior expert for resolving the grievance.

Note: this ordinance provides paper showing and grievance handling only. No re-evaluation or revaluation provision was found in this ordinance.

## 15. Eligibility for passing

Regime note: Autonomous Ordinance 2024-25 — autonomous batches only; not the 2023–27 Affiliated Batch (AKTU rules).

Passing standards for B.Tech / MBA / MCA programs:

- Theory / blended courses: End Semester Examination (ESE) minimum 30%, and overall assessment (ESE + CIE) minimum 40%.
- Lab / internship / project / viva-voce / field work courses where only CIE is proposed: overall assessment (CIE) minimum 50%.
- Lab / internship / project / viva-voce / field work courses where both CIE and ESE are proposed: overall assessment (ESE + CIE) minimum 50%.
- Non-credit courses and skill enhancement courses (such as Foreign Language, IKS, UHV, Communication Skills, Innovation and Entrepreneurship, Campus to Corporate): overall assessment (ESE + CIE) minimum 40%.

For M.Tech, theory courses require ESE minimum 40% and overall (ESE + CIE) minimum 50%.

Non-credit courses have only five grades: A+ Outstanding, A Excellent Performer, B Good Performer, C Average Performer, F Fail. Students who do not satisfy the criteria, or who remain absent, are deemed to have failed in that course (a backlog) and are awarded FF or AB grades respectively.

## 16. Grading system

Regime note: Autonomous Ordinance 2024-25 — autonomous batches only; not the 2023–27 Affiliated Batch (AKTU rules).

At the end of each semester, students are awarded a letter grade in each registered course. A relative grading system on a 10-point scale is followed; based on the marks range, grade cut-offs are decided and a proper bell curve is maintained. For each course, the average grade point is maintained at 7.

Grade points: A+ 10 Outstanding, A 9 Excellent, B+ 8 Very Good, B 7 Good, C+ 6 Satisfactory, C 5 Average, D 4 Pass, FF 0 Fail, AB 0 Absent in End Semester Examination, DT — Detained (no grade point awarded). A student who obtained grades A+ to D is considered passed. FF and AB grades mean the course is a backlog course: an FF grade means the student failed in the evaluation and must reappear for the examination; an AB grade is a backlog awarded when a student is absent in the End Semester Examination. A DT grade is awarded when a student is detained in a course; no grade point is awarded.

SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average) is the ratio of the sum of the products of credits and grade points scored in all courses registered in the semester to the sum of the credits of all courses in that semester, including courses with F or AB grades. CGPA is calculated the same way over all semesters. SGPA and CGPA are rounded off to 2 decimal places. The formula for conversion of CGPA into percentage of marks is: CGPA x 10 = percentage of marks. So CGPA 5.00 = 50%, 6.00 = 60%, 6.50 = 65%, 7.00 = 70%, 7.50 = 75%, 8.00 = 80%.

Students promoted to the third semester onwards with CGPA less than 5.00 are given a chance to improve their CGPA above 5.00 by appearing in a maximum of 10% of the total credits, improving the grade point only by the end semester examination of the same course, as per the examination rule.

### Promotion rules (B.Tech)

There is no restriction for promotion from an odd semester to the next even semester. For promotion from an even semester to the next odd semester (the next higher academic year), the student must have earned minimum credits: registration to Semester III requires 50% of total credits (rounded off to the lower side) of Semester I + II; registration to Semester V requires 50% of total credits of Semester III + IV (for diploma holders, 50% of total credits of Semester III + IV); registration to Semester VII requires 50% of total credits of Semester V + VI. For example, where there are 45 credits for a year and 50% of credits is 22.5 credits, the eligibility is 22 credits.

## 17. Declaration of results

Semester examination results are declared within 15 days after the last theory/practical examination by the CoE office. At the end of each semester, students are issued a Grade Card by the Result Section / Registrar's office indicating the grades secured in each course, SGPA and the latest CGPA. No representations regarding the results are accepted after three months from the result declaration date. If an examinee does not rectify discrepancies in their examination registration application within two months of the result declaration, their registration to the examination may be cancelled.

## 18. Vacation exam

Students with backlogs (only FF grades) get the benefit of vacation examinations. It is compulsory for all students to appear for the end semester examination. An examinee who does not pass after appearing in the end semester examination is eligible to reappear in the vacation examination. The vacation examination is an extension of the end semester examination and is later considered an attempt. It is conducted only for students with backlogs in the just-preceded end semester examination; failure students of the ESE should fill the form of the vacation examination on the notified date and time. It is conducted similar to the ESE, with centralised evaluation of answer sheets. Grades are awarded by substituting the end semester marks with the marks secured in the vacation examination. Cut-off limits remain the same as the main examination. Students with an FF grade in any course due to unfair-means (UFM) cases in the ESE are not permitted to appear in the vacation examination of that academic session.

## 19. Summer semester

The summer semester supports students who were detained in a few courses during the regular ESE examinations (odd or even semester) due to low attendance. It is offered at an accelerated pace, with the course load double that of a normal semester (for example, a one-credit course requires two hours of class per week). If a student's attendance is less than 90% in any course/tutorial, they are not permitted to take the summer semester examination of that course.

- Only students detained in a few courses of the odd and even semesters can register.
- The summer semester is generally for theory/blended/practical category courses, not for audit/non-credit courses.
- In the summer semester, students may register for a maximum of 16 credits including both semesters, and must submit a "Consent to Enroll" for joining.
- For students who register for the summer semester, all CIE marks secured in previous attempts stand cancelled; the summer semester is considered a new semester for the registered courses.
- Fees for the summer semester are decided by the Finance Committee.
- Once registered, students are not allowed to withdraw from a summer semester.
- After attaining 90% attendance in the summer semester, students are eligible to appear for summer semester examinations, which are treated as a re-conduction of the end semester examinations of the just-concluded academic year.
- The summer semester is not applicable to courses not registered in the previous odd and even semester.
- The summer semester is a special semester, and students cannot demand it as a matter of right. It is mandatory for students to appear for regular examinations for all courses in the regular semester, as the summer semester may not be offered for all courses. A minimum batch size is required, and offering it should not be considered a precedent.
- If a student is detained in more than 16 credits, they can register for those courses in the summer semester of the next academic session.

## 20. Carry-over exam

Students who could not clear all backlogs during vacation examinations but are promoted to the next higher academic year as per the promotion criteria should appear in that theory/lab/project course during the respective end semester examinations of subsequent academic sessions in which they got a backlog (FF grade). A student with a backlog due to absence in the end semester examination (AB grade) or a UFM case, but promoted as per the promotion criteria, should appear in that course during the respective end semester examinations of subsequent academic sessions. Students appearing for carry-over papers in any semester are examined with the examination paper of that course running in that semester. However, CIE marks awarded at the previous attempt in the courses concerned are carried forward.

## 21. Not promoted to higher academic year

If the promotion criteria are not fulfilled even after vacation examinations or summer semester examinations, the student is not promoted to the next higher academic year. The following rules apply:

- EX-STUDENT: if the student is not promoted to the higher academic year and has only backlog courses with no detained courses, the student may appear in the end semester examinations of the next academic year as an EX-STUDENT for all courses of the previous academic year. In such cases, CIE marks awarded at the previous attempt in all the courses are carried forward.
- The student may be allowed re-admission. In such cases, CIE marks awarded at the previous attempt in all the courses are cancelled. The student pays the fees for the whole academic year, attends classes and appears in all examinations.
- If the student is not promoted to the higher academic year and has a few detained courses, they must take re-admission.

## 22. Re-admission in the institution

A student may be allowed re-admission provided they satisfy one of these conditions: (i) the student is not promoted to the next higher academic year as per the promotion criteria in section 21; (ii) the student has not registered for any semester during an academic year; (iii) the student has detained courses and is not promoted to the next academic year; (iv) the student did not register for any semester of an academic year, did not appear in an End Semester Examination, or was not granted permission to appear in the End Semester Examination.

## 23. Special provision for scribe / writer

Students who use a scribe must produce a medical certificate in the prescribed form from a Medical Officer in Government Service, to the effect that the student has physical limitations to write, including speed, and submit it along with the Admit Card at the time of examination. A disability certificate must be issued by the Medical Board / Doctor of not below the rank of Civil Surgeon / Medical Superintendent of a Central or State Government Hospital / Medical College, certifying the nature (permanent or temporary) and percentage of disability and its duration. The academic qualification of a scribe (writer) should be one step lower than the qualification possessed by the student, and at minimum matriculate or above. Every student with a specified disability, whether using a scribe or not, is entitled to 30 minutes extra time for every 3 hours of examination.

## 24. Semester drop

If a student is unable to attend classes for more than four weeks in a semester due to serious illness, they may apply to the concerned Dean for withdrawal from the semester. Such a request, with necessary supporting documents, must be made to the concerned Dean latest before the start of the End Semester Examination. Partial withdrawal from the semester is not allowed. All rules and regulations for promotion apply.

## 25. Award of division, ranks and medals

A student is eligible for the award of a degree/certificate from the Institute and the University provided they have completed all requirements for the program, paid all dues to the Institute including the Department, Hostels, Library and other units, and have no case or disciplinary action pending against them.

For B.Tech: after successful completion of 160 credits and securing a CGPA of 5.00 or above, a student is eligible for a B.Tech degree. Division and CGPA are awarded only after the eighth and final semester examination, based on integrated performance. B.Tech First Division requires CGPA 7.5 and above; B.Tech Second Division requires CGPA 5.00 up to 6.0. B.Tech with Honors (First Division with Honours) requires CGPA 7.5 and above, plus 20 or more credits of NPTEL MOOC courses or Global Certifications plus Project, with the student passing each course of that degree program in a single attempt without any grace marks and without any gap.

## 26. Awards

Awards are given for excellent performance in academics, sports, cultural and extracurricular activities, debates, etc. The Gold Medal is awarded to the outstanding engineering graduate (topper amongst all branches) and to the best postgraduate student. The Silver Medal is awarded to the most outstanding graduate (topper) in each branch of Engineering. The Achiever's Award goes to the student whose performance is best in academic as well as extra-curricular, co-curricular and other activities taken together (best male outgoing and best female outgoing). Topper's Award: 1st position Rs. 12,000, 2nd position Rs. 6,000 (merit-based scholarship for program/branch toppers, judged on both semesters' results without carryover, clear pass in first attempt, awarded in the following year). Students of B.Tech and B.Pharm 1st to 3rd year securing 85% aggregate marks (including both semesters) are awarded Rs. 500, plus Rs. 200 for each percentage point above 85% (for example, a student securing 90% is awarded Rs. 1,500).

## 27. Unfair means

Cases of unfair means are dealt with as per the Unfair Means (UFM) Policy of the Institute for Mid Semester Examinations and End Semester Examinations (Annexure C of the ordinance).

## 28. Issue of duplicate marksheet / TC

Duplicate mark sheet / TC is issued for fees prescribed by the finance committee from time to time; processing time is 4-5 days.

## 29. Issue of transcript

After successful completion and award of the degree, a transcript may be issued. Candidates can apply for transcripts by submitting an application to the Registrar office along with photocopies of the grade sheets, the name of the University where they wish to apply, and the prescribed fees. Processing time is 4-5 days.

## 30. Passing certificate / provisional degree

Students who have completed all requirements are eligible for the issue of a Passing Certificate / provisional certificate during the graduation day ceremony of the institution, duly signed by the CoE and Head of the Institution.

## 31. Award of degree and certificate

After successful completion of the program, the student is eligible for the award of the degree in a subsequent convocation of the Institute, provided they have passed all prescribed courses, attained the minimum required CGPA, satisfied the minimum academic requirements, satisfied all requirements specified by the concerned department and by the Academic Council and/or ordinances, paid all dues to the institute, and have no pending case of indiscipline.
